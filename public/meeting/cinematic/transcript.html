Learning to act

Athena
You have seen the world. Now let us look at the intelligence inside it. Mira, what are we actually teaching?

Dr. Mira Chen
We are teaching a policy: a way to choose an action from what the agent can observe. Picture Rick at the edge of the prison yard. He has a position, a mission, and information about the world around him. He chooses an action. The world changes. He receives feedback. During training, experience like this is used to update the policy. Over many attempts, the learning process searches for decisions that produce better outcomes over time. That is reinforcement learning. The important phrase is over time. Moving quickly can look useful in the moment, but a mission may depend on waiting for a teammate. Collecting supplies can look like success, but the job includes getting home. We design the environment, the available actions, the observations, and the rewards. The agent practices within that structure. Our engineering task is to make the structure express the behavior we actually want.

The Unity mission

Athena
Rowan, take us back through that mission. Show us why three useful individuals still need to learn how to work as a team.

Rowan Vale
Start at the prison depot. The team has two outward routes, a sheltered waystation, and a supply cache in the barn. Rick carries the retrieval role. Scout has the wider view and reports the safer route. Negan has the protective role. Those abilities create dependencies. Information has to become a decision. A decision has to become coordinated movement. The team must regroup, collect the supplies, return, and bring every survivor home. This footage is our scripted demonstration of that intended mission. It lets you see the world and the behavior we are asking the learning system to develop. The value of Unity is that we can make this problem concrete. We can see positions, change conditions, repeat a run, and record the events. The environment gives an abstract learning problem a place where decisions have visible consequences.

The research foundation

Athena
That gives us a mission we can see. Mira, give our shareholders the engineering foundation behind it. What can we build on today?

Dr. Mira Chen
We have a working Unity environment, three specialized role policies, and a record of what happens in each run. We verified the mission mechanics across twenty-four scripted test configurations. We also produced sixteen complete scripted practice trips to demonstrate the intended behavior. Those demonstrations give the learning process examples to learn from. Each of the three role policies reached a saved checkpoint of just over five hundred thousand training steps. The training combines reinforcement learning with behavior cloning from those demonstrations. These are different parts of the foundation: the mechanics, the examples, and the trained checkpoints. Our dashboard keeps them distinct. We can save a team, hold its policy fixed, repeat a defined test suite, and inspect its route reports, mission milestones, and outcomes. End-to-end reliability is the next research milestone. What we have built is the environment and the measurement system that make that progress testable. That turns the project into a repeatable engineering process.

The AEGIS vision

Athena

Elias Ward
Welcome to the proving ground. In this vision, the compound is a connected learning environment. Cargo vehicles move supplies between a receiving hangar and the teams that need them. Maintenance crews inspect equipment. A control room watches the condition of the site. Before a change reaches the physical operation, its decision process can be rehearsed in a simulation. A vehicle encounters a blocked lane. A delivery is delayed. A piece of equipment needs attention. We ask the same question you saw in Grayhaven: can local decisions add up to a successful shared mission? The setting is larger, but the underlying challenge is familiar. Information is incomplete. Resources are limited. Actions affect what happens next. Reinforcement learning gives us a way to explore policies for those sequential decisions. The proposed value is better preparation: more useful practice, clearer comparisons, and fewer surprises when people make decisions about the real operation.

Practice under change

Athena
A compound cannot depend on every day looking the same. How does the training ground prepare a team for conditions it has not rehearsed exactly?

Elias Ward
We vary the conditions and keep the mission understandable. In the AEGIS scenario, a supply vehicle might begin from a different position, a route might take longer, or a service bay might be unavailable. We want to see how the policy responds when the easy pattern changes. Some conditions belong in practice. Others are reserved for later tests. Keeping those separate helps us judge whether a behavior extends beyond the examples it already saw. The simulation is a place to rehearse and compare. A physical compound introduces new conditions, new constraints, and human responsibilities. Moving into that setting would require additional engineering and validation. That is why the vision includes people supervising the system and deciding where it is ready to be used. Learning supports the operation through a sequence of tested steps. The objective is dependable support for the people working inside the compound.

Choosing the objective

Athena
Mira, the system will practice what we reward. How do we make sure a useful-looking number corresponds to a useful mission?

Dr. Mira Chen
Begin with the outcome. For Supply Run, finding the cargo is one milestone. The complete objective includes returning with the supplies and the whole team. That distinction changes how we interpret progress. A policy can make one part of a task look impressive while leaving the larger job unfinished. In our future compound, the same reasoning applies to a delivery schedule or a maintenance queue. Speed is meaningful alongside completion, resource use, and the constraints people set. A reward is a design choice. It is our attempt to express an intention in a signal the learning process can use. We then inspect the behavior that signal produces. We also keep a separate evaluation: clear conditions, frozen policies, and outcomes we can compare. Training tells us what the system experienced. Evaluation tells us what the saved system did in a defined test. Shareholders should be able to ask for both. That is how evidence becomes part of the product we are building.

The work behind the world

Athena
Rowan, bring this back to the people building it. What does a productive day of reinforcement-learning work actually look like?

Rowan Vale
It starts with a specific question. We build or adjust a scenario, check that its rules work, and decide what evidence would answer the question. Then we run the experiment and inspect what happened. Sometimes the next useful change is an observation. Sometimes it is a reward, a demonstration, or a better test condition. The work is connecting those choices to behavior. Dane and his project partner direct that process: choosing the experiments, checking the results, and deciding what to improve. Unity makes the world visible. The training system makes practice repeatable. Sentinel makes the record inspectable. Together they give us a way to move from an idea, to a test, to a decision about the next piece of engineering.

Why it matters

Athena

Elias Ward
Look for a process where today's decision changes tomorrow's options. A warehouse team chooses how to sequence work. A service operation decides which task should happen next. A utility system balances changing demand against available resources. These are examples of decision problems where a learning approach might be worth investigating. In our AEGIS world, those ideas come together in logistics, maintenance, energy, and training. The ambition is a compound whose supporting systems can be rehearsed together before people depend on them. Whether a learning method is useful will depend on the task, the quality of the simulation, the alternatives, and the evidence. Sometimes a simpler method will be the right choice. The business opportunity is the ability to make that comparison well: to test an idea, understand its tradeoffs, and spend effort where it produces a meaningful improvement. That is a practical engineering capability with applications beyond this fictional world.

The next chapter

Athena

Dr. Mira Chen
The next chapter is stronger coordination across the complete mission. We can target practice at the transitions between stages, improve demonstrations where they are useful, and test the saved team against the same defined conditions. As the behavior develops, we can broaden the conditions and compare it with a matched baseline. The questions stay concrete. Does the team complete the mission? Does it stay together? Does the behavior hold when the setup changes? We record the answers and use them to choose the next experiment. The longer-term AEGIS vision builds outward from that discipline: richer simulations, more interconnected decisions, and carefully evaluated applications. A compelling world gives people a reason to pay attention. A repeatable research process gives us a reason to keep building. Our investment case today is that foundation: a working environment, specialized agents, and an expanding ability to test what intelligent teamwork can become.
